06 Daily Learning Log Template
// Copy this into your notes app — fill it every session
DATE:
[ date ]
SERIES:
[ Mastery Plan · Topic 60–82 ] or [ DSA · D1–D40 ]
TOPIC:
[ topic number and name ]
WHAT I COVERED:
2–3 sentences in your own words. Not copied from notes. Your own language.
WHAT CLICKED:
The one thing that finally made sense. Be specific — not "I understood signals", but "I understood WHY computed() re-runs only when its dependencies change."
WHAT IS STILL UNCLEAR:
Be specific. Vague confusion cannot be resolved. "I don't understand effects" → "I don't understand why catchError must be inside switchMap not outside it."
APPLIED AT WORK TODAY:
Any concept from this week's topics that appeared in your actual work code. Even noticing it counts.
TOMORROW'S INTENTION:
Exactly what you will cover next session. One sentence. Specific enough that tomorrow-you knows exactly where to start.
★ The "Still Unclear" field is the most important line in this log. It becomes the first thing you resolve in your next session — not something you carry forward indefinitely. This is the habit that separates developers who retain knowledge from those who feel like they are constantly relearning the same things.

08 The 5 Non-Negotiable Rules
  • Never move the gym

    It is your physical and mental recovery. Every developer who cuts recovery to study more ends up burning out and stopping entirely. The gym is not negotiable — it is what makes the rest of this sustainable.

  • Commute time is not wasted time

    Your 2 hours of daily commute on office days is 10 hours per week of learning time that most developers throw away on social media. Protect it with the same seriousness as a scheduled meeting.

  • The WFH morning block is sacred

    Do not let it drift to 10am. Do not let Slack pull you in early. Set a hard boundary: 8:00–9:30am is learning time. Your employer gets you from 10am. Those 90 minutes are the highest-value learning time in your entire week.

  • One topic per session, fully understood

    Never rush to the next topic because you finished early. If you finish with 20 minutes remaining — spend those 20 minutes writing it out in your own words or attempting the exercise again from scratch. Depth over speed, always.

  • Friday is never a new topic day

    Friday is always review and consolidation. This is the rule that prevents the "I learned 10 things this week and retained none of them" problem. Review what you covered. Fix what is unclear. Only then are you ready for next week.

10 The Missed Day Protocol
The only thing that derails this plan is not difficulty — it is skipping one day, feeling guilty, and then skipping another to avoid the guilt.
The rule for a missed day: Acknowledge it in your log. Write one sentence: "Missed [date] — reason: [honest reason]." Start the next session exactly where you left off as if nothing happened. No catching up. No doubling sessions. No guilt spiral. Just continue. The chain is not broken by one missed day. It is broken by the decision to stop trying after a missed day.
